Atlas ERP — Solution Architecture Notes

Revision 7, 2026-08-20. Maintained by the solution architect. This page is exported from the programme wiki and is the reference description of how Atlas is put together. Where it disagrees with an interface specification, the interface specification wins.

1. Context

Atlas replaces two systems: the finance ledger in use since 2014 and the warehouse management system in use since 2011. Both are retired at their respective cutovers. Nothing else in the estate is replaced by this programme, and in particular the CRM platform is out of scope and is being migrated separately under the Orion programme.

1.1 Deployment model

Atlas is a vendor-hosted single-tenant deployment in the eu-west region, with an application tier the vendor operates and a reporting replica the delivery organisation operates. The reporting replica is read-only and lags the transactional store by up to fifteen minutes.

2. Modules

2.1 Finance

General ledger, accounts payable, accounts receivable, and fixed assets. Period close runs against the transactional store, never the replica, which is why replica lag does not affect the close. Cutover is milestone M2.

2.2 Warehouse

Inbound receipting, putaway, picking, and stock adjustment. The warehouse module shares the journal import path with finance, so a change to that path is a change to both modules. Cutover is milestone M3.

3. Integration architecture

3.1 Inbound interfaces

Seven inbound interfaces are in scope: supplier master, purchase orders, goods receipts, payments, payroll journals, bank statements, and the daily FX rate feed. All seven are file-based except payments, which is a synchronous API provided by the second integration vendor.

Payments is the only interface with a real-time dependency on a third party, and it is therefore the only interface whose failure mode is an outage rather than a delayed batch. That is why the payments regression harness is treated as cutover-critical and why the unconfirmed vendor test window is carried as a delivery risk rather than a scheduling inconvenience.

3.2 Outbound interfaces

Three outbound interfaces: the consolidated trial balance to the group reporting tool, the stock position to the demand planning tool, and an audit extract to the archive. All three are nightly batch files.

3.3 The legacy payments bridge

A bridge that accepts the legacy payment file format and translates it to the Atlas payments API. It exists so that cutover does not depend on the payments interface being ready, and it is licensed separately at approximately 6,000 USD per month. The plan is to retain it for two weeks after the M2 cutover and then decommission it.

4. Data migration

4.1 Approach

A full historical migration of seven years of ledger data, run as a sequence of rehearsals against a copy of production, followed by a final run inside the cutover freeze. Each rehearsal produces a reconciliation report; the acceptance tolerance is 10 unresolved exceptions.

4.2 The cost centre mapping problem

Cost centres retired in the 2024 restructure were never added to the migration ruleset, so historic journal entries carrying them fail reconciliation. This is the root cause of 31 of the 41 exceptions produced by the second rehearsal and is fixed by an extension to the mapping table rather than by a change to the migration engine.

6. Open architecture decisions

One decision remains open: whether the audit extract is produced by the application tier or by the reporting replica. Producing it from the replica is cheaper and is the current assumption, but it makes the extract subject to replica lag, which the internal audit function has not yet accepted.
