# Atlas ERP Rollout - Q3 2026 Budget Summary

RESTRICTED - FINANCE GROUP ONLY. Prepared by the programme management office on
2026-08-31 from the finance ledger extract of the same date. Figures are in USD
and exclude recoverable VAT. This document is the authoritative budget position
for the Atlas programme; the monthly delivery status report quotes headline
figures from it and is not a substitute for it.

## 1. Headline position

The approved budget for the Atlas programme is 480,000. Actual spend to
2026-08-31 is 292,800, or 61.0% of approval. The forecast at completion is
515,000, an overrun of 35,000, or 7.3% above approval.

Elapsed schedule at the reporting date is 68% of the baseline duration, so the
programme is spending marginally slower than it is consuming schedule. That
comparison is not a health indicator on its own: the remaining schedule contains
the two cutovers, which are the most expensive weeks in the plan.

The entire forecast overrun sits in three categories, and one of them dominates.
Vendor professional services carries 27,000 of the 35,000.

## 3. What drives the overrun

### 3.1 Vendor professional services

Two unplanned migration rehearsals were run in July and August at a combined
cost of 19,000. A third rehearsal, if the steering committee funds it, adds a
further 11,000 at weekend rates, of which 8,000 is not yet in the forecast
because the decision has not been taken. Extended vendor support hours during
the August rehearsal account for the remaining 8,000 of the variance.

### 3.2 Data migration tooling

The reconciliation reporting module of the migration tool was licensed for three
months and is now required for five, because the rehearsal cycle has extended.
The additional two months cost 2,500.

### 3.3 Contingency

Contingency has been drawn down by 1,200 to date. The forecast assumes a further
19,300 is drawn, which is the whole of the remaining balance plus the 5,500
increase requested. Contingency at this level provides no cover for a fourth
rehearsal or for an extension of the legacy payments bridge beyond two weeks.

## 4. Commitments not yet invoiced

Purchase orders raised and not yet invoiced total 41,700. Of that, 28,000 is the
vendor professional services order covering the cutover weekend, 9,200 is the
migration tooling extension, and 4,500 is training delivery already performed
and awaiting invoice. These commitments are included in the forecast at
completion and excluded from actual spend to date.

## 5. Forecast method

The forecast at completion is built bottom-up from remaining planned work, not
by extrapolating the burn rate. Each remaining work package is priced at its
contracted rate, commitments are added at their purchase order value, and
contingency is forecast at the balance the programme expects to draw rather than
at the balance remaining. A burn-rate extrapolation at the reporting date would
have produced 431,000 and would have been wrong by 84,000, because it cannot see
the cutover weekends.

## 6. Sensitivities

| Scenario | Effect on forecast |
| Third rehearsal funded and clears | plus 11,000, M2 recovered |
| Third rehearsal funded and fails | plus 11,000, M2 still late |
| Legacy payments bridge retained a further month | plus 6,000 |
| Fourth rehearsal required | plus 11,000 and contingency exhausted |
| Warehouse cutover runs to plan | no change; already fully forecast |

The worst realistic case, a funded third rehearsal that fails followed by a
fourth, takes the forecast to 537,000 and exhausts contingency entirely.

## 7. Governance position

The delegated tolerance for this programme is 5% of approval, or 24,000. The
forecast overrun of 35,000 exceeds it, so the overrun cannot be absorbed by a
steering committee decision and requires a change request to the investment
board. The steering committee instructed the PMO lead on 2026-08-27, under
decision SC-2026-08-02, to prepare a change request for 40,000, being the
forecast overrun plus a 5,000 contingency uplift.

Until that change request is approved, no further spend may be committed against
the categories carrying variance, which in practice means the third rehearsal
cannot be ordered without either board approval or an explicit sponsor waiver.

## 9. Assumptions and exclusions

### 9.1 Assumptions

The forecast assumes the contracted vendor day rate holds to the end of the
programme, that no further migration rehearsal beyond the third is required,
that the legacy payments bridge is decommissioned two weeks after the M2
cutover, and that internal delivery staff remain at their current allocation
through to M3. Each of these assumptions is testable at the September steering
committee and none of them has changed since the July report.

### 9.2 Exclusions

Excluded from this budget: recoverable VAT, the cost of internal business users
attending training, the hardware refresh in the warehouse depots, and any
remediation arising from the internal audit review of the audit extract. The
last of these is the only one with a realistic chance of becoming a cost in this
financial year, and it would fall to the audit function budget rather than to
the programme.

### 9.3 Currency and rounding

All figures are in USD. Vendor invoices denominated in EUR are converted at the
rate in force on the invoice date and are not revalued afterwards. Totals are
rounded to the nearest 100, so a column may not sum exactly to its total.
